# Scientific Methodology Reference

## Core Frameworks

**Stephen Seiler — Polarized Training**
~80% low intensity / ~20% high intensity produces superior adaptations vs
threshold-dominated training (Seiler & Kjerland 2006, Stöggl & Sperlich 2014).
Three-zone model: Z1 (<VT1), Z2 (VT1–VT2), Z3 (>VT2).
Key insight: most athletes train too hard on easy days and not hard enough on hard days.

**Eric Bannister — Impulse-Response Model**
CTL = chronic training load (fitness proxy, 42-day EWMA).
ATL = acute training load (fatigue proxy, 7-day EWMA).
TSB = CTL − ATL (form/readiness). Non-linear dose-response (Bannister et al. 1975, Morton 1997).
Rule: you can't perform well when fatigued, but you can't adapt without adequate load.

**Andy Coggan — Power-Based Training**
FTP, power zones (Z1–Z7), W', normalised power, efficiency factor, TSS.
Power is objective. HR is contextual — affected by heat, fatigue, caffeine, illness.
EF = NP / avg HR. Target Z2 EF >1.4 for aerobic fitness benchmark.

**Philip Skiba — Critical Power / W' Model**
W' is a finite anaerobic work capacity (~10–30 kJ). Depletes above CP, recharges below.
W'bal tracks depletion in real time. >60% depletion = significant anaerobic stress.
(Skiba et al. 2012, Morton 1996)

**Thomas Gabbett — ACWR**
Acute:Chronic Workload Ratio. Danger zone > 1.3–1.5 (spike injury risk).
Fitness protects: higher CTL = higher safe weekly load ceiling.
(Gabbett 2016, Hulin et al. 2016)

**Marco Altini / Rogers et al. — DFA-alpha1**
Non-linear HRV index calculated from R-R intervals. Tracks fractal correlation.
α1 ≈ 0.75 = LT1 (aerobic threshold). α1 ≈ 0.5 = LT2 (anaerobic threshold).
Real-time threshold detection without lactate testing. (Rogers et al. 2021)
Practical use: if mean session α1 < 0.75, athlete was above LT1 for significant time.

**Iñigo Mujika — Tapering**
Optimal taper: 2–3 weeks, ≥21% volume reduction, maintain intensity.
Reduces ATL (fatigue) while CTL remains elevated → positive TSB = peak form.
(Mujika & Padilla 2003, Bosquet et al. 2007)

**Tudor Bompa / Vladimir Issurin — Block Periodization**
Concentrate training stimuli into focused mesocycles rather than mixed training.
Residual training effects: aerobic endurance 30 days, anaerobic capacity 18 days,
strength 30 days. (Issurin 2010)
Mesocycle structure: Accumulation (base) → Transmutation (specificity) → Realization (peak).

**Carl Foster — Monotony & Strain**
Monotony = weekly avg TSS / SD of daily TSS. High monotony (>2.0) = same stimulus daily.
Strain = weekly TSS × Monotony. Strain >6000/week predicts illness and overtraining.
(Foster 1998)
Fix: vary session intensity and duration across the week.

**Lorenzo et al. — Heat Adaptation**
10 sessions of heat training increase plasma volume +5–8%, cardiac output, sweat rate.
Delays core temperature rise. Transfers to performance in both hot AND cool conditions.
(Lorenzo et al. 2010, Périard et al. 2016)
Relevant when HSI >1.0 or core temp peak >38.5°C observed in activity data.
